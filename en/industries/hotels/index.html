Industries

Hotels

Hotel operations connect many guest moments, departments and handovers. Especially in seasonal properties, the quality of interfaces determines whether standards survive high occupancy.

Field notes

Operational pressure points

  • Seasonal teams need to reach a common operating level in a short period.
  • F&B and front office influence guest information, expectations and complaint handling.
  • Multilingual teams need standards that are linguistically simple and operationally unambiguous.
  • Peak weeks test handovers, breakfast, bar, banqueting and leadership at the same time.

Operations

What the review looks at

The review focuses on the guest journey and handovers between reservations, front office, housekeeping and F&B where relevant to scope. With seasonal staff, onboarding, briefing and role coverage become central levers. For F&B, available data such as occupancy, guest mix, average spend or sales mix is connected to actual shift routines. Standards need to be concise enough to survive staff turnover.

Operational detail

Particular attention goes to the interfaces between departments. Guests do not experience an organisation chart; they experience one hotel. Information lost between reservations, reception, housekeeping and F&B quickly becomes a problem in the guest journey.

With seasonal teams, we review how quickly new employees can understand their role, where issues are escalated and how shift handovers work. Standards need to be simple in language and short enough to remain usable at peak occupancy.

Figures such as occupancy, average spend and sales mix only become useful when connected to the real operating flow. This makes it easier to distinguish a capacity problem from a process problem and to set the right priority.
