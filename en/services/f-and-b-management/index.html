F&B Management

An F&B concept works only when menu, costing, purchasing, production, service and positioning fit together. Attractive ideas do not fix poor product cost or slow routines on their own.

For whom

  • Hotels that want to manage F&B as a clearer profit and experience function
  • Restaurants with uncertain menu profitability, food cost or production logic
  • New concepts that need to test offer, pricing architecture and operations before launch

Operating problem

How the work is structured

The review uses available POS data, sales mix, product cost, portion and recipe standards, plus the flow between purchasing, storage, kitchen, bar and service. Menu engineering connects popularity and contribution rather than focusing only on headline bestsellers. Changes are tested against production reality, staffing needs and guest perception.

Operational detail

F&B decisions are reviewed from three perspectives at once: guest, production and numbers. An offer can look attractive while remaining operationally weak if purchasing, preparation, storage and service do not work together.

Sales mix, food cost, recipes and portion standards are considered alongside the real production flow. POS data shows what sells; conversations with kitchen and service reveal where bottlenecks form and how workable the offer really is.

The objective is to align offer, price and operating capacity. When the recipe, purchasing unit, portion and selling price follow the same logic, the calculation becomes reliable enough for day-to-day management.

Decisions in daily operations

F&B decisions also need to account for operational capacity. An item with a strong theoretical margin may be unattractive at peak if it creates a bottleneck at the pass, consumes disproportionate mise en place or requires too much explanation from service. The same applies to bar drinks with long production times. Product mix, production steps and the selling situation are therefore reviewed together. Stock variances, waste and unclear portion sizes are treated as process questions. A calculation becomes genuinely manageable only when recipe, purchasing unit, portion and selling price use the same logic.

Output

Deliverables

  • Menu-engineering analysisAnalysis

Duration & format

Format: analysis, concept work and implementation support. Data can come from POS, purchasing, recipes and stock counts where available. Duration: depends on project scope.

FAQ

F&B Management

Does the entire menu need to be rebuilt?

No. A targeted reduction, revised pricing logic or better production planning can be more effective than starting again.

Do you also work with bar offers?

Yes. For bars we also review recipe accuracy, pouring standards, speed, inventory and the structure of the cocktail menu.
